Set the rate once, let the time turn into line items, and see at a glance who still owes you.

Rates per client, rounding per project.

  1. Each client keeps its own rate. Change it once and every new entry follows.
  2. Round up to 6, 15 or 30 minutes — or don’t round at all. The raw time is kept either way.
  3. Bill in the client’s currency. Your own reports stay in yours.

The invoice writes itself.

  1. Tracked time becomes line items, grouped by project. Edit any line before sending.
  2. Your client pays from a link. No account, no app to install.
  3. The due date follows the client’s payment terms.

Know who owes you, without a spreadsheet.

  1. Overdue invoices rise to the top and turn coral. Nothing else on the screen does.
  2. A polite reminder goes out three days after the due date. You write the wording once.
  3. When the money lands, the invoice marks itself paid.

Questions people ask first

Do my clients need an account?

No. They get a link to the invoice and can pay or download the PDF without signing up for anything.

Can I bring my old time entries?

Yes — import a CSV with a date, client, description and duration. The column names don’t have to match; you map them once and Tallyhour remembers.

What if I forget to stop the timer?

A timer that runs past ten hours pauses itself and asks you about it the next morning. Fix the entry and the invoice uses the corrected time.

Which currencies can I bill in?

Any. Each client has its own currency; your reports convert everything to yours at the rate of the invoice date.

What happens if I cancel?

You drop to Solo. Every invoice, PDF and export stays downloadable — nothing is deleted.

Is Tallyhour a real company?

No — it’s a concept project. The design and the working demo at the top of the page are real; the business behind them isn’t.
